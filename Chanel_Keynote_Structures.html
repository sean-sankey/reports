One controlling idea

Eight talks where a single idea carries the whole thing, with the three strands underneath it and the shape of the arc.

Top two for the room

Top pickExplicit

Atul Gawande, Cowboys and Pit Crews

Harvard Medical School commencement, 2011 · outside tech

We train and pay doctors to be cowboys, and what people actually need is a pit crew.

Three strands
  1. See whether the system is succeeding
  2. Design the fixes
  3. Spread them at scale

Plus three values replacing autonomy: humility, discipline, teamwork.

Arc. Falls on complexity outrunning any one expert, and the cost in avoidable harm. Rises into the pit crew. Closes with a real cowboy who turns out to work off checklists, landing the image twice.

Why this one. One picture holds the whole argument, exactly like breaking in a boot. Far enough from digital Europe that nobody argues about the content instead of the shape.

Top pickExplicit

Kathy Sierra, Building the Minimum Badass User

Business of Software, Boston, 2012

Nobody wants your product. They want to be brilliant at the thing your product is for. So compete on user awesome, not app awesome.

Three strands, numbered by her
  1. Worked examples of excellence
  2. Deliberate practice, from unreliable to reliable
  3. A visible road from novice to mastery

Arc. Falls on the feature arms race, and on users who quit because they feel stupid. Rises through each of the three gifts. Ends with the user as the hero, not the product.

Why this one. Thirteen people about to talk about their own products. Her idea flips that into what the audience can do afterwards, and her three are genuinely numbered.

Tech and digital

Explicit

Bret Victor, Inventing on Principle

CUSEC, Montreal, 2012

Creators need an immediate connection to what they make, and finding a principle worth serving is a way to live, not just a way to build.

Three strands
  1. His own principle, shown through live demos
  2. Others who worked to a principle: Tesler, Engelbart, Kay, Stallman
  3. How you find one of your own

Arc. Falls on the gap between writing code and seeing it, then on careers that drift by default. Rises through each demo, then again into work as activism. Ends on choice.

Explicit

Satya Nadella, Build keynote

San Francisco, 2016

Human language is the new interface, so conversation itself becomes the platform we all build on.

Three strands
  1. Conversations between people
  2. Conversations with a personal digital assistant
  3. Conversations with bots as a new kind of application

Arc. Opens on a fall about technology's consequences and the duty that comes with it. Rises through the three layers, each demonstrated. Ends by handing the work to developers.

Explicit

Tristan Harris and Aza Raskin, The AI Dilemma

San Francisco, March 2023

We are racing to deploy a technology we do not understand, and the race, not the technology, is the danger.

Three strands, stated as rules
  1. A new technology creates a new class of responsibility
  2. If it confers power, it starts a race
  3. If the race is not coordinated, it ends badly

Arc. The deepest fall here, built in stages: first contact with social media, then second contact with generative systems. The rise is narrow and late. Coordination, slower release, liability.

Inferred

Maciej Ceglowski, The Website Obesity Crisis

Web Directions, Sydney, 2015

The web has grown fat, and the weight is not features. It is surveillance and vanity, carried on the reader's back.

Three strands, compressed by us from his eight sections
  1. Fat ads
  2. Fat assets
  3. Heavy clouds

Arc. Falls hard and repeatedly, each section fresh evidence. One rise at the end: keep it simple and the web stays something people can build in, not only consume.

Inferred

Steve Jobs, Macworld Boston keynote

Boston, 1997 · the Microsoft investment one, not the iPhone

Apple does not win by Microsoft losing. Apple wins by being very good at what only Apple can do.

Three strands, compressed from his five
  1. Stop fighting the wrong war
  2. Invest where we are already strong
  3. Partner rather than posture

Arc. Opens in the fall, a company near death. The Microsoft announcement is a second deliberate fall, with the room booing on camera. It rises straight out of that boo into the end of the competition era, and closes on who Apple is for.

Inferred

David Foster Wallace, This Is Water

Kenyon College commencement, 2005

The freedom worth having is the freedom to choose what you pay attention to, and what things mean.

Three movements, not his own frame
  1. The two fish
  2. The atheist and the Eskimo
  3. The supermarket and the traffic on a bad day

Arc. A long, deliberately grinding fall through the default setting and the tedium of adult life. It rises only at the very end, into attention as a choice, and lands on the four words it began with.

Rejected, and useful because of it

Rory Sutherland, Perspective is everything, TED 2011

Clear idea about perceived value, but the body is a run of unconnected examples with nothing organised beneath it. The counter-example: a strong controlling idea still leaves an audience with nothing to carry if the strands are missing.

Jensen Huang, GTC and CES keynotes

Widely praised, structurally a product catalogue. There is a theme, accelerated computing, but a theme is not a controlling idea, because nothing in it can be argued with.
